title: Quantifiers with countable and uncountable nouns
kind: usage

Countable nouns can be counted and pluralised: a book, two books. Uncountable nouns cannot:
water, information, advice, furniture, money, news, progress, luggage, research.

- **many / a few / fewer** with countable: many books, a few friends, fewer cars
- **much / a little / less** with uncountable: much water, a little time, less traffic
- **a lot of / lots of / plenty of** work with both
- **some / any** in statements, offers and negatives

Common tricky pairs:

- "How many money?" -> "How much money?"
- "I have many informations." -> "I have a lot of information."
- "He gave me good advices." -> "He gave me good advice."
- "There are many furnitures." -> "There is a lot of furniture."
- "I need some new luggages." -> "I need some new luggage."
- "Fewer people than last year" (countable) vs "less traffic than last year" (uncountable)

Words that look plural but are singular: news, mathematics, physics, economics, politics.

Words that look singular but behave as plural: people, police, cattle.

Partitive expressions make uncountables countable: a piece of advice, a slice of bread, a
bottle of water, a bit of news, an item of furniture.

Learner errors worth catching in speech: *many* + uncountable, *informations/advices*
as plurals, and *less* + countable. Recast with the same quantity word and keep talking:
"Right, I have a lot of information about that too, actually."
