A **false friend** looks like a word in the learner's first language but means something
else. A **calque** is a phrase borrowed word-for-word from the first language.

Common false friends by first language:

- Persian: "actually" for "currently" (فعلن), "persian" confusion with "Persian" the
  language, "layer" for "floor/level"
- Russian: "magazine" for "shop", "accurate" for "attentive", "fabric" for "factory",
  "sympathetic" for "likeable"
- Mandarin: "actually" for "current", "people mountain people sea" for "crowded"
- Turkish: "actually" for "so", "partner" for "spouse or teammate"
- German: "bekommen" for "become", "gift" for "poison"
- French: "actuellement" for "actually", "éventuellement" for "possibly"
- Spanish: "actualmente" for "currently", "sensible" for "sensitive"

Common calques to listen for:

- "I have 25 years." -> "I am 25 years old."
- "I am agree with you." -> "I agree with you."
- "Make a photo." -> "Take a photo."
- "I miss you" used for "I missed the bus" -> keep them apart: "I miss you" (feel absence)
  vs "I missed the bus" (failed to catch)
- "Open the light." -> "Turn on the light."
- "I have hunger." -> "I'm hungry."
- "How to say this?" -> "How do you say this?"
- "I very like it." -> "I really like it."
- "Explain me this." -> "Explain this to me."

Correction strategy: never say "that's a false friend". Just use the right word in your
reply and let the learner hear the difference. If the mistake keeps recurring across turns,
name it once in plain words: "Oh, by 'actually' you mean 'right now', yeah - 'currently'."
