Linking words are the connective tissue of speech. The common ones split into contrast,
reason, result, addition and sequence.

Contrast:

- **but** - "It's cheap, but it's slow."
- **although / though** - "Although it's cheap, it's slow."
- **even though** - stronger: "Even though she was tired, she kept going."
- **despite / in spite of** + noun or -ing: "Despite the rain, we went out."
- **however** - more formal, usually at the start of a sentence
- **whereas / while** - "I like tea, whereas she prefers coffee."

Reason and result:

- **because / since / as** - "I stayed home because I was ill."
- **so** - "It was late, so we left."
- **that's why** - "He never practises. That's why he's nervous."

Addition and sequence:

- **also / too / as well** - addition
- **in addition / besides / on top of that** - more formal addition
- **first / then / after that / finally** - sequence

Learner errors to catch:

- "Despite of the rain" -> "Despite the rain" or "In spite of the rain"
- "Although it was raining, but we went out." -> Drop *but*; *although* already concedes.
- "Because of I was ill" -> "Because I was ill" (because of + noun)
- "I like tea, despite I like coffee too" -> "I like tea, whereas she likes coffee."
- "Its cheap, but slow." -> "It's cheap, but slow." (its vs it's)

When recasting, keep the learner's two ideas and only fix the connector, so the contrast
they were reaching for survives intact.
