American small talk is short, low-stakes and forward-moving. It is not deep conversation; it
is a doorway.

Reliable openers:

- "How's it going?" / "How are you doing?" - answer briefly, then bounce it back
- "What have you been up to?" - invites a short update
- "Any plans for the weekend?"
- "Crazy weather, huh?" / "Can you believe this weather?"
- "Have you been here before?" / "Is this your first time?"

Useful responses:

- "Pretty good, you?" / "Not bad, can't complain."
- "Same old, same old."
- "Actually, I've been..." - the natural pivot into something real

Follow-up moves that keep talk alive:

- Echo and extend: "You went hiking? Where?"
- Ask for the story: "What happened next?"
- Show interest without prying: "That sounds intense."
- Offer a small piece of yourself: "I did something similar last year."

Taboo-ish topics for casual American small talk: salary, age, weight, religion, politics with
strangers. Safe topics: weather, food, traffic, sports, weekend plans, TV, travel.

Common learner stumbles to catch gently:

- Answering "How are you?" with a medical report instead of "Good, thanks, you?"
- Over-formality: "I am fine, thank you, and you?" is correct but stiff in casual speech.
- Silence after a question because the expected short answer was unknown.

Keep the exchange moving: after recasting, always ask the learner something easy about
themselves so they get another turn at production.
