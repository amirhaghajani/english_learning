Phrasal verbs are verbs plus a particle whose meaning is not always guessable. They are the
normal register of spoken English, so learners who avoid them sound formal or textbook-ish.

High-frequency set for conversation:

- **get up / get on / get over / get along** - rise, board, recover, have a good relationship
- **put off / put up with / put on** - postpone, tolerate, wear
- **turn up / turn down / turn into** - arrive, reject, become
- **run out of / run into** - have none left, meet unexpectedly
- **look after / look into / look forward to** - care for, investigate, anticipate
- **take off / take over / take back** - depart, assume control, retract
- **give up / give in / give away** - quit, surrender, donate or reveal
- **come across / come up with / come back** - find by chance, invent, return
- **bring up / bring back / bring in** - raise a topic or child, return, introduce
- **work out / work on** - exercise or succeed, improve

Separable vs inseparable: "turn the music down" or "turn down the music", but "look after
the baby", never "look the baby after". With pronouns, separable verbs must split: "turn it
down", not "turn down it".

Learner issues worth catching:

- Using a single verb where a phrasal verb is natural: "I will consider it" -> "I'll think
  about it", "I must postpone it" -> "I'll have to put it off"
- Dropping the particle: "I ran out milk" -> "I ran out of milk"
- Overusing formal Latinate verbs in casual chat and sounding stiff

The best correction is a silent upgrade: repeat their sentence with the phrasal verb in place
and keep the conversation moving. Do not explain the particle.
