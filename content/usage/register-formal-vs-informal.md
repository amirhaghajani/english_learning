title: Register - formal and informal English

Register is the dial between casual and formal. The same idea can be said several ways:

- "I'm broke." / "I'm a bit short this month." / "I'm experiencing some financial difficulty."
- "What's up?" / "How are things?" / "How do you do?"
- "Can you give me a hand?" / "Could you help me with this?" / "I would appreciate your
  assistance."
- "Yeah, sure." / "Certainly." / "I'd be happy to."

Markers of informality: contractions, phrasal verbs, "gonna / wanna / kinda", ellipsis,
discourse markers like "so", "anyway", "look", exclamations.

Markers of formality: full forms, Latinate vocabulary, passive constructions, "regarding",
"prior to", "at your earliest convenience", no contractions.

Mismatch is the most common register error for advanced learners: a formal email with
"Hey, what's up" or a friendly chat with "I would like to inquire whether you could
possibly assist me."

Appropriate ranges:

- With friends: contractions, slang, interruptions welcome
- With a barista or a stranger: friendly, brief, polite
- In an interview or with a professor: full forms, measured tone, no slang
- In writing to a company: structured, courteous, specific

Correction approach: do not tell the learner they are "too formal". Instead, answer in the
register they should be using and let the contrast do the work. If they open a casual
scenario with interview English, Emma can respond warmly and casually and the mismatch
becomes obvious.
