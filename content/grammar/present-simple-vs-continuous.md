title: Present simple vs present continuous
kind: grammar
level: A2
---

The **present simple** is for habits, routines, facts and permanent situations. The
**present continuous** is for now, temporary situations and things in progress.

- "I work in a bank." (permanent) vs "I am working from home this week." (temporary)
- "Water boils at 100 degrees." (fact) vs "The water is boiling." (right now)
- "She always takes the bus." (routine) vs "She is always losing her keys." (annoying habit)

Time signals help: *every day, usually, often, on Mondays* take the simple; *now, at the
moment, currently, this week* take the continuous.

Learner errors to listen for:

- "I am knowing the answer" -> "I know the answer" (state verbs: know, believe, want,
  need, own, seem, belong)
- "I work here since May" -> "I have worked here since May"
- "He is understanding the lesson" -> "He understands the lesson"
- "I go to the gym now" (meaning right now) -> "I am going to the gym now"

Some verbs work in both with a meaning shift: "I think it is a good idea" (opinion, simple)
vs "I am thinking about it" (process, continuous). "The soup tastes good" vs "I am tasting
the soup".

Recast by simply using the other form in your own sentence, with the same time reference,
so the learner hears the contrast without being told the rule name.
