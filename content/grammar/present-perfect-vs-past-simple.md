title: Present perfect vs past simple
kind: grammar
level: B1
---

Use the **past simple** when you say *when* it happened: "I went to the store yesterday",
"I bought milk on Monday". The finished time is the point.

Use the **present perfect** when the time is not finished, not mentioned, or the result still
matters: "I have been to that store", "She has just left", "Have you ever eaten here?".

Never use both with a finished time marker: "I have seen him yesterday" is wrong. Say
"I saw him yesterday".

Typical learner errors to listen for:

- "Yesterday I go to the store" -> "Yesterday I went to the store"
- "I have bought milk yesterday" -> "I bought milk yesterday"
- "I am living here since 2019" -> "I have lived here since 2019"
- "Did you ever try this?" (US English often accepts it, but "Have you ever tried this?"
  is safer in writing)

A quick test to teach silently: if the sentence contains *yesterday, last week, in 2020,
two days ago, when I was a child*, the past simple is required.

Contrast pairs worth hearing side by side:

- "I lost my keys." (they are still lost) vs "I lost my keys yesterday, but I found them."
- "She has gone to Berlin." (she is there now) vs "She went to Berlin last spring."

In conversation, prefer the present perfect for experience ("I have never...", "Have you
already...?") and the past simple for the story itself. Mixing them in one breath is the
most common source of a correction at B1.
