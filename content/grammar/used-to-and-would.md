title: used to and would for past habits
kind: grammar
level: B2
---

**Used to** describes past habits or states that are no longer true:

- "I used to smoke." (I don't now)
- "She used to live in Tehran." (she doesn't now)
- "There used to be a cinema here."

**Would** also describes repeated past actions, but never past states:

- "Every summer we would drive to the coast." (repeated action) - correct
- "I would live in Tehran." (past state) - wrong; use "used to"

Negative and question forms drop the *d*: "I didn't use to like olives", "Did you use to
play an instrument?"

For present habits that are unusual, English uses **will**: "She will sit for hours doing
puzzles." That is a different structure and rarely needed at conversation level.

Learner errors to listen for:

- "I use to go there." -> "I used to go there."
- "I am used to live alone." (meaning accustomed) vs "I used to live alone." (past habit)
- "I used to living in a small town." -> "I used to live in a small town."
- "When I was child, I would be very shy." -> "When I was a child, I was very shy."

The *be used to + -ing* pattern means "be accustomed to": "I am used to getting up early."
Confusing it with *used to + base verb* is one of the clearest signals that a learner is
reaching for a new structure, so acknowledge the attempt and recast it in the form they
probably meant.
