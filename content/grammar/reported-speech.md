When you report what someone said, the tense usually moves one step back and the pronouns
and time words shift.

- "I am tired," she said. -> She said she **was** tired.
- "I saw him yesterday," he said. -> He said he **had seen** him **the day before**.
- "I will call you," she said. -> She said she **would** call me.
- "Where do you live?" he asked. -> He asked where I **lived**.

Backshift is optional when the statement is still true: "She said she is a doctor" is fine.

Reporting verbs matter for tone: *said, told, asked, explained, admitted, suggested, warned,
promised*. *Tell* needs a person: "He told me", not "He said me".

Yes/no questions become *if/whether*: "Do you like it?" -> She asked if I liked it.

Common learner errors:

- "He said me that..." -> "He told me that..."
- "She asked where do I live." -> "She asked where I lived."
- "He said he will come." -> "He said he would come."
- "I asked him what time is it." -> "I asked him what time it was."

In conversation, the highest-value catch is word order inside the reported question, because
learners keep the question inversion. Recast the whole reported clause naturally: "Right,
she asked me where I lived, and I said I'd moved last spring."
