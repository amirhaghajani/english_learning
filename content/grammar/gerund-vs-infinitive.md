title: Gerund or infinitive after a verb
kind: grammar
level: B1
---

Some verbs are followed by *-ing*, some by *to + base*, and a few change meaning.

Verb + gerund: enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, practise, keep, give up, look forward to,
be used to, can't help.

- "I enjoy cooking." / "She avoided answering." / "He kept talking."

Verb + infinitive: want, need, decide, hope, expect, plan, promise, agree, refuse, learn,
would like, can't afford.

- "I want to leave." / "She decided to stay." / "They refused to pay."

Meaning changes:

- "stop doing" (quit) vs "stop to do" (pause in order to)
- "remember doing" (recall) vs "remember to do" (don't forget)
- "forget doing" (not recall) vs "forget to do" (fail to do)
- "try doing" (experiment) vs "try to do" (attempt, difficult)
- "go on doing" (continue) vs "go on to do" (then do the next thing)

Prepositions are always followed by a gerund: "interested in learning", "good at cooking",
"looking forward to seeing you", "instead of waiting".

Common learner errors:

- "I enjoy to read." -> "I enjoy reading."
- "She wants going home." -> "She wants to go home."
- "I am looking forward to meet you." -> "I am looking forward to meeting you."
- "He suggested to go." -> "He suggested going."

When recasting, repeat the learner's sentence with the other pattern and add a short
follow-up question in the same shape so the correct form lands twice.
