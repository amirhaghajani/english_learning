title: Subject-verb agreement
kind: grammar
level: A2
---

A singular subject takes a singular verb in the present simple; a plural subject takes the
base form.

- "She works late." / "They work late."
- "The news is good." (news is singular)
- "My family is small." (US English; British English often "My family are small.")
- "Everyone is here." (indefinite pronouns take singular verbs: everyone, someone, nobody,
  each, either, neither)

The -s ending is the most common written and spoken slip for learners at A2-B1, especially
in fast speech where the final sound is easy to drop.

Typical corrections:

- "He go to work by train." -> "He goes to work by train."
- "My brother don't like coffee." -> "My brother doesn't like coffee."
- "There is many people here." -> "There are many people here."
- "The children is playing." -> "The children are playing."
- "I has a question." -> "I have a question."

Agreement across a long subject is a classic trap:

- "The box of chocolates are on the table." -> "is on the table"
- "A list of names are missing." -> "is missing"

Question and negative forms carry the tense on the auxiliary, so the main verb loses its -s:
"She doesn't play tennis", not "She doesn't plays tennis".

In speech, correct by re-saying the learner's clause with the right ending and moving on.
Do not stop to explain the third-person -s rule unless the learner asks.
