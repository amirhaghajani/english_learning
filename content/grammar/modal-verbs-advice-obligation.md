title: Modal verbs for advice, obligation and possibility
kind: grammar
level: B1
---

Modals are followed by the base verb and never take *-s* or *to* (except *ought to*).

Advice and recommendation:

- **should / shouldn't** - "You should see a doctor."
- **ought to** - slightly more formal, same meaning
- **had better** - strong, often with a warning: "You'd better leave now."

Obligation and permission:

- **must** - speaker's authority: "You must submit it today."
- **have to** - external rule: "I have to wear a uniform."
- **mustn't** - prohibition: "You mustn't use your phone here."
- **don't have to** - no obligation: "You don't have to come."
- **can / could / may** - permission, *may* being the most formal

Possibility and deduction:

- **might / may / could** - "It might rain."
- **must** - strong deduction: "She must be home by now."
- **can't** - negative deduction: "That can't be right."

Learner errors to catch:

- "You must to go." -> "You must go."
- "He can swims." -> "He can swim."
- "I mustn't go" (meaning no obligation) -> "I don't have to go"
- "I should to call her." -> "I should call her."
- "She can to drive." -> "She can drive."

The *mustn't* vs *don't have to* confusion is worth catching because it reverses the
meaning. Recast by stating the rule as you understand it: "So you don't have to come, but
you mustn't be late."
