title: Conditionals zero, first and second

Four shapes cover most conversation:

- **Zero** - always true: "If you heat ice, it melts." (if + present, present)
- **First** - real future possibility: "If it rains, we will stay in." (if + present, will)
- **Second** - unreal or unlikely now: "If I had more time, I would learn the guitar."
  (if + past, would + base)
- **Third** - unreal past: "If I had known, I would have called you." (if + past perfect,
  would have + participle)

Common learner errors:

- "If I will have time, I will call you." -> "If I have time, I will call you."
  No *will* in the if-clause.
- "If I would be rich, I would travel." -> "If I were rich, I would travel."
- "If I would have known..." -> "If I had known..."
- "Unless I don't hurry, I'll miss it." -> "Unless I hurry, I'll miss it."
  *Unless* already carries the negative.
- "If I was you, I would..." is common in speech; "If I were you" is the safer written form.

Teaching move: repeat the learner's sentence with the fix inside your own reply, then ask a
second-question that needs the same structure. For example, if they say "If I will see him,
I will tell him", answer "Sure, if I see him first, I'll tell him. Would you tell him
yourself if he called?"

Keep the distinction light in speech: most learners at B1 are aiming for the first and
second conditional, and mixing *will* into the if-clause is the thing worth catching.
