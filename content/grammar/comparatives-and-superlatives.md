Short adjectives take **-er / -est**: "cheaper", "the cheapest", "bigger", "the biggest".
Longer ones take **more / most**: "more expensive", "the most expensive".

Spelling notes: double the final consonant after a short vowel (big -> bigger, hot -> hotter),
change *y* to *i* (happy -> happier), and drop the *e* (nice -> nicer).

Irregulars worth hearing: good -> better -> best, bad -> worse -> worst, far -> further ->
furthest, little -> less -> least, many/much -> more -> most.

Never use both markers together:

- "more cheaper" -> "cheaper"
- "the most cheapest" -> "the cheapest"

Comparison structures:

- "as ... as": "This is as fast as the old one."
- "less / least": "It is less noisy than I expected."
- "than" after a comparative: "She is taller than her brother."
- "the ... , the ...": "The sooner we leave, the better."

Common learner slips to catch in conversation:

- "He is more taller than me." -> "He is taller than me."
- "This is the most good restaurant." -> "This is the best restaurant."
- "My English is more good now." -> "My English is better now."
- "It is more easier." -> "It is easier."

When recasting, keep the learner's adjective and just fix the marker, then push one step
further with a natural comparison of your own so they hear the pattern twice.
