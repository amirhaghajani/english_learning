Time:

- **at** for clock times and fixed points: at 7 o'clock, at noon, at night, at the weekend
  (British), at Christmas
- **on** for days and dates: on Monday, on 3 May, on my birthday
- **in** for months, years, seasons, parts of the day: in July, in 2024, in winter,
  in the morning

Place:

- **in** for enclosed spaces and areas: in the room, in London, in a queue
- **on** for surfaces and lines: on the table, on the wall, on the second floor, on the bus
- **at** for a point: at the door, at the corner, at the station

Movement uses **to**: "I go to work", "She came to the party". But "I am at work" for
location. And "arrive in" a city/country, "arrive at" a building.

Transfer problems to listen for:

- Speakers of Russian, Persian, Turkish, Hindi and Mandarin often choose prepositions by
  literal translation.
- "I am in the bus" (meaning riding it) -> "I am on the bus"
- "I will call you in Monday" -> "I will call you on Monday"
- "I live in the London" -> "I live in London"
- "I go to home" -> "I go home"
- "I am boring" (meaning not interested) -> "I am bored" (a different problem entirely:
  -ing vs -ed adjectives)

Do not chase every preposition. Correct the ones that make a listener pause, and recast by
repeating the whole phrase correctly in your reply rather than isolating the word.
