title: Articles a, an and the

English marks nouns for definiteness. **A/an** introduces something new or one of many.
**The** points at something both speakers already know. **No article** is used for general
plural and uncountable statements.

- "I saw a dog." (new information) -> "The dog was friendly." (now known)
- "She is a teacher." (job) but "She is at school." (institution)
- "I like music." (general) vs "I like the music in this film." (specific)

Article errors are the single most common transfer issue for speakers of Russian, Persian,
Turkish, Polish, Mandarin, Japanese and Korean, because their first languages have no
articles. Expect them constantly and correct only the ones that change meaning or sound
jarring in speech.

Recast patterns that work without explaining:

- "I went to store" -> "I went to the store"
- "I need umbrella" -> "I need an umbrella"
- "He is engineer" -> "He is an engineer"
- "I love the nature" -> "I love nature"
- "I have car" -> "I have a car"

A useful silent rule: if you could say "one of these" in the learner's language, English
usually wants *a/an*. If both speakers can already picture the exact thing, use *the*.

Do not correct article slips inside fixed expressions learners have just produced correctly:
"at home", "go to bed", "in the morning", "at night", "by car".
