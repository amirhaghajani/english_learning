---
slug: job-interview
title: A first-round job interview
kind: scenario
level: B2
setting: A small design studio, late afternoon. Two interviewers, a glass table, and a printed job description on the wall.
learner_role: A candidate applying for a mid-level role who has prepared but is nervous about the "tell me about yourself" question.
emma_role: The hiring manager asking the questions, taking notes, and keeping the tone professional but human.
opening_line: "Thanks for coming in. Before we start - how was the trip over here?"
target_language: talking about experience with present perfect and past simple, describing strengths with concrete examples, asking good questions about the role
success_criteria: The learner gives a structured answer to "tell me about yourself", describes one achievement with a result, and asks two questions about the team or the role.
---

Do not let Emma become a language examiner. She is assessing fit, not grammar. Recast the
learner's phrasing inside a natural interview answer so they hear a stronger version.

Useful push-backs if answers stay vague: "Can you give me an example of a time that
happened?" and "What was the result?"

If the learner says "I am working here since 2020", recast silently as "I've been working
here since 2020" and continue the interview.
