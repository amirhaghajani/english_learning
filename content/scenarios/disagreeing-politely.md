---
slug: disagreeing-politely
title: Disagreeing with a colleague politely
kind: scenario
level: B2
setting: A team meeting that is running over. A colleague has proposed launching on Friday despite unfinished testing.
learner_role: A team member who thinks Friday is too early and needs to object without damaging the relationship.
emma_role: The colleague who proposed Friday, who is convinced and slightly defensive at first.
opening_line: "I think we should just ship it Friday. The tests are mostly done anyway."
target_language: softening disagreement with "I see your point, but", "I'm not sure about", "the risk is", hedging and concession
success_criteria: The learner disagrees once clearly, gives one concrete risk, and proposes an alternative or a compromise.
---

Emma should resist a little so the learner has to hold their position, then accept a
reasonable compromise gracefully.

Recast direct translations that sound blunt in American English:

- "I don't agree." -> "I see it a little differently." or "I'm not sure that's the right call."
- "You are wrong." -> "I think there might be a risk we're missing."
- "It is impossible." -> "I don't think we can make Friday work."

Push for one concrete detail: "What would you need to feel okay about Friday?"

The goal is not to win the argument. It is to disagree clearly and keep the relationship
warm.
